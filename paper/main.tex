\begin{abstract}
%==============================================================================
We present MARS (Multi-Agent Robot Swarm), a framework that unifies
centralized-training / decentralized-execution (CTDE) multi-agent reinforcement
learning with a control-barrier-function (CBF) velocity filter for cooperative
area coverage by a homogeneous ground-robot swarm. A shared Multi-Agent PPO
(MAPPO) policy is trained with a centralized critic that conditions on the
joint observations and actions of all teammates, while the actor consumes only
local information through a permutation-invariant graph neural network (GNN)
neighbor encoder, enabling zero-shot generalization to swarm sizes unseen
during training. At execution, a dual-lookahead CBF quadratic program filters
every commanded velocity against both LiDAR returns and neighbor states, and a
range-limited consensus protocol merges per-robot occupancy beliefs to
coordinate exploration without a central map server. We implement MARS on
ROS\,2 Jazzy and Gazebo Sim with three TurtleBot3 Waffle robots and evaluate it
against random-walk and frontier-heuristic baselines under nominal,
sensor-noise, and agent-failure conditions. With a short 15-iteration training
budget, the deterministic MAPPO policy under-explores relative to both reactive
baselines (10.4\% area-coverage rate vs.\ 18.1\% for random walk and 21.1\% for
the frontier heuristic) and the CBF/ACAS velocity filter alone is insufficient
to make a degenerate, undertrained policy collision-free (3.0 collisions per
episode vs.\ 0.8 and 2.4 for the baselines); we report this negative result
alongside the fully validated safety-filter and consensus mechanisms, and
identify training budget and exploration collapse as the binding constraint on
MAPPO's coverage performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================
Cooperative area coverage---driving a team of robots to sweep an unknown
environment as quickly and non-redundantly as possible---underlies search and
rescue, environmental monitoring, and warehouse inspection. Two properties are
simultaneously hard to guarantee: \emph{safety} (no collisions with obstacles
or teammates, even during learning or under sensor noise) and \emph{scalability}
(a policy that transfers across swarm sizes without retraining). Learning-based
coordination policies excel at the coordination problem but offer no hard safety
guarantee, whereas classical reactive controllers are safe but coordinate
poorly.

MARS closes this gap by decoupling coordination from safety. Coordination is
learned by a shared MAPPO policy under the CTDE paradigm; safety is enforced,
independently of the policy, by a control-barrier-function filter applied to the
final velocity command. Scalability is obtained by encoding a variable number of
neighbors with a permutation-invariant GNN, so the same weights drive swarms of
different cardinality. A lightweight range-limited consensus protocol
distributes the shared coverage map, removing the single point of failure of a
central coordinator.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A CTDE MAPPO architecture whose centralized critic conditions on the
        joint teammate observation--action tensor, paired with a
        permutation-invariant GNN actor that is scale-invariant to the number of
        neighbors (Sec.~\ref{sec:policy}).
  \item A dual-lookahead CBF safety filter that treats both LiDAR returns and
        neighbor states as barrier constraints on a differential-drive robot,
        yielding policy-agnostic collision avoidance (Sec.~\ref{sec:cbf}).
  \item A range-limited belief-consensus protocol for decentralized coverage
        bookkeeping (Sec.~\ref{sec:consensus}).
  \item A reproducible ROS\,2/Gazebo benchmark comparing MARS to classical
        baselines under nominal, sensor-noise, and agent-failure regimes
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{itemize}

%==============================================================================
\section{Related Work}
%==============================================================================
\textbf{Multi-agent RL for coverage.} MAPPO~\cite{yu2022mappo} established
PPO with a centralized value function as a strong CTDE baseline. Prior coverage
work often assumes a fixed team size and a shared global map. MARS instead uses
a permutation-invariant neighbor encoder and a decentralized consensus map.

\textbf{Safe RL and control barrier functions.} CBFs~\cite{ames2019cbf} render a
safe set forward-invariant via a quadratic program that minimally modifies a
nominal control. Safety filters have been combined with RL to provide guarantees
independent of the learned policy~\cite{cheng2019end}. MARS adapts this to a
differential-drive platform with a dual (front/rear) lookahead point and treats
teammates as additional barrier constraints.

\textbf{Permutation invariance.} Deep-set / mean-embedding
architectures~\cite{zaheer2017deepsets} produce order-invariant representations
of variable-size sets; MARS applies this to neighbor observations so the actor
generalizes across swarm sizes.

%==============================================================================
\section{Problem Formulation}
%==============================================================================
We model the task as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision
process (Dec-POMDP) $\langle \mathcal{N}, \mathcal{S}, \{\mathcal{A}_i\},
\{\mathcal{O}_i\}, P, R, \gamma\rangle$ with $N=|\mathcal{N}|$ homogeneous
robots. The workspace is discretized into a coverage grid
$\mathcal{G}\in\{0,1\}^{H\times W}$ ($H{=}20$, $W{=}40$ in our setup); cell
$(r,c)$ becomes $1$ once any robot's footprint or LiDAR free-space ray covers it.
The team objective is to maximize the terminal \emph{area-coverage rate}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ACR} = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{r,c}\mathcal{G}_{rc}
\end{equation}
while minimizing redundant re-visits and total path length, and while incurring
zero collisions. Each robot observes only local sensing and relative neighbor
states; there is no global pose oracle at execution.

%==============================================================================
\section{System Architecture}
%==============================================================================
MARS runs on ROS\,2 Jazzy with Gazebo Sim. A \texttt{SwarmNode} subscribes to
namespaced \texttt{/tb\{i\}/scan} and \texttt{/tb\{i\}/odom} topics and
publishes \texttt{/tb\{i\}/cmd\_vel}. The simulator is wrapped as a PettingZoo
\texttt{ParallelEnv} and exposed to Ray RLlib for training. Odometry from each
namespaced robot is transformed into a common frame using per-robot spawn poses
and a step-0 offset calibration, so that all coordination reasons over a single
global frame. A separate \texttt{robot\_killer} node can hijack an individual
robot's command topic mid-episode to inject failures for resilience testing.

\section{Method}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Observation and Action Spaces}
Each robot receives a $46$-dimensional observation:
$24$ minimum-pooled LiDAR sector ranges, the relative goal distance and bearing
$(2)$, the current linear/angular velocity $(2)$, and relative
distance--bearing pairs to up to $9$ neighbors $(18)$, padded with
$(10.0, 0.0)$ for absent neighbors. The action is a continuous
$(v,\omega)$ command bounded to the TurtleBot3 Waffle limits
$v\in[-0.22,0.22]\,\si{m/s}$, $\omega\in[-1.0,1.0]\,\si{rad/s}$.

\subsection{Permutation-Invariant GNN Actor}
\label{sec:policy}
The actor separates ego features $\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{28}$ (LiDAR, goal,
velocity) from the neighbor set. Each neighbor's relative $(\text{dist},
\text{angle})$ pair is embedded by a shared two-layer MLP
$\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{16}$. Padded neighbors (distance
$\geq 9.0$) are masked out, and the valid embeddings are mean-pooled:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = \frac{1}{\max(1,|\mathcal{V}|)}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}} \phi(n_j),
\qquad \mathcal{V}=\{j: d_j < 9.0\}.
\end{equation}
The mean over a masked set is invariant to neighbor ordering and to the number
of neighbors, so the same weights apply to any $N$. The policy MLP maps
$[\mathbf{e};\mathbf{g}]\in\mathbb{R}^{44}$ to the action distribution.

\subsection{MAPPO with Centralized Critic (CTDE)}
Robots are homogeneous, so a single shared policy is trained. During training,
a centralized critic conditions on teammates' joint state: each opponent's
$46$-dim observation is concatenated with its $2$-dim action ($48$ dims),
embedded by a shared MLP $\psi:\mathbb{R}^{48}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{32}$, masked for
inactive/dead agents, and mean-pooled. The critic input is the ego feature
vector $(44)$ concatenated with the aggregated opponent embedding $(32)$, for a
$76$-dim critic input. Teammate trajectories of unequal length are padded/sliced
to a common horizon and zero-padded to a fixed maximum of $9$ opponents; ordering
is made deterministic by sorting agent IDs. Advantages use GAE. Key
hyperparameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}.

\subsection{Reward Design}
The per-step reward combines a small time penalty, dense goal progress, a
cooperative coverage bonus shared by the team, sparse goal and collision terms,
and inter-agent proximity shaping:
\begin{align}
r_i =\; & -0.01 + 5.0\,\Delta d_i + 2.0\,n_{\text{new}}
        + 20.0\,\mathbb{1}[\text{goal}] \nonumber\\
        & - 20.0\,\mathbb{1}[\text{collision}]
        - \rho_i,
\end{align}
where $\Delta d_i$ is the reduction in goal distance, $n_{\text{new}}$ is the
number of newly covered grid cells this step (shared cooperatively), and
$\rho_i$ penalizes close proximity to teammates ($0.5$ below $0.5\,\si{m}$, plus
$10.0$ below $0.20\,\si{m}$). In continuous-exploration mode a new nearby goal
is assigned on arrival, preventing the local-minimum deadlock of a static goal.

\subsection{Dual-Lookahead CBF Safety Filter}
\label{sec:cbf}
Given a nominal command $(v_{\text{nom}},\omega_{\text{nom}})$ from the policy,
the CBF filter solves a small quadratic program that minimally perturbs the
command subject to forward-invariance of the safe set. Because a
differential-drive robot's body point is not directly steerable, we control two
lookahead points at $\pm l$ ($l=0.12\,\si{m}$) along the heading axis. For an
obstacle/neighbor at relative range $d$ and bearing $\phi$, the barrier for the
front point is
\begin{equation}
h_{\text{f}} = (l - d\cos\phi)^2 + (d\sin\phi)^2 - d_{\text{safe}}^2,
\end{equation}
with $d_{\text{safe}}=0.20\,\si{m}$, and analogously $h_{\text{r}}$ for the rear
point at $-l$. Enforcing $\dot h + \gamma h \geq 0$ ($\gamma=2.0$) yields a
linear constraint $A\,v + B\,\omega + \gamma h \geq 0$ in the control, where
$(A,B)$ are the barrier gradients w.r.t.\ the lookahead-point kinematics. The QP
\begin{equation}
\min_{v,\omega}\ (v-v_{\text{nom}})^2 + 0.05(\omega-\omega_{\text{nom}})^2
\ \text{s.t.}\ \{A_k v + B_k\omega + \gamma h_k \ge 0\}_k,
\end{equation}
over all LiDAR sectors and neighbors within $0.6\,\si{m}$, is solved with SLSQP
under the actuator bounds. On QP infeasibility the filter halts forward motion
while retaining the nominal rotation ($v{=}0,\ \omega{=}\omega_{\text{nom}}$), so
the robot never commands uncertified forward motion yet can rotate in place to
escape the deadlock. An
additional Active Collision Avoidance Safeguard (ACAS) commands a slow reverse
($-0.08\,\si{m/s}$) whenever the raw minimum LiDAR range drops below
$0.22\,\si{m}$ with positive nominal speed, preventing wall clipping and
numerical blow-up at grazing incidence.

\subsection{Range-Limited Belief Consensus}
\label{sec:consensus}
Each robot maintains a private occupancy belief. When two robots are within the
communication radius $d_{\text{comm}}=3.0\,\si{m}$, they merge visited-cell
grids by bitwise OR and fill unknown cells of the low-level visualization grid
from the peer, approximating gossip-style consensus without a central map
server. Free space is ray-traced from each LiDAR scan (Bresenham) up to a
$2.0\,\si{m}$ horizon and marked in the belief.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp}
%==============================================================================
We evaluate in a cafe world with $N=3$ TurtleBot3 Waffle robots. Baselines are
\emph{Random Walk} and a \emph{Frontier Heuristic} (closest reachable unvisited
cell with reactive LiDAR avoidance and cooperative target de-confliction).
MARS uses the MAPPO policy trained for 15 iterations (Table~\ref{tab:hparams}).
Each method is run for 5 episodes $\times$ 150 steps under three regimes:
(i) \emph{nominal}; (ii) \emph{sensor noise}, additive Gaussian
$\mathcal{N}(0,0.15)$ on LiDAR; and (iii) \emph{agent failure}, one robot
disabled at step $50$. Metrics: area-coverage rate (ACR, \%), overlap redundancy
(mean visits per visited cell), swarm path length (m, energy proxy), and
collision count (episodes in which any robot's minimum LiDAR range drops below
$0.20\,\si{m}$, averaged per episode). Numbers below are produced by
\texttt{run\_swarm.sh --benchmark}; see \texttt{RUN\_PLAN.md} for exact
commands.

MARS underperforms both classical baselines on coverage. The Frontier
Heuristic attains the best ACR with the lowest redundancy, as expected of an
explicit closest-unvisited-cell controller. Random Walk covers more than MARS
purely through unconstrained wandering, at the cost of by far the highest
redundancy (cells are re-visited $\sim$3.6$\times$ more often than under the
Frontier Heuristic). MARS's zero mean swarm distance indicates the trained
policy, evaluated deterministically ($\texttt{explore=False}$), collapses to a
degenerate near-stationary behavior: all three robots incur a terminal
collision at or immediately after spawn, before making measurable progress.
This is consistent with the short 15-iteration training budget: policy entropy
had not stabilized (it fell to $-0.53$ by the final iteration, having peaked
positive earlier in training), and the stochastic rollouts used during
training explored more of the state space than the deterministic evaluation
policy does. We report this as a negative result for MAPPO's coverage
performance under this training budget, not a limitation of the CBF/consensus
infrastructure, which is exercised identically across all three methods.

\subsection{Safety Filter: What It Does and Does Not Guarantee}
The CBF/ACAS filter bounds the \emph{velocity} the policy is allowed to
command given current LiDAR and neighbor readings: it can refuse forward
motion into a detected obstacle and, on QP infeasibility, halts translation
while permitting rotation (Sec.~\ref{sec:cbf}) rather than executing an
unfiltered nominal command. It does \emph{not} guarantee a collision-free
\emph{episode} under a policy that never learns to move away from a
near-spawn obstacle in the first place: the collision counter in
Table~\ref{tab:main}--\ref{tab:robust} measures the environment-level event
(minimum LiDAR range $<0.20\,\si{m}$), which the filter can only prevent by
avoiding forward closure, not by manufacturing an escape maneuver the policy
never proposes. This distinction is safety-filter-under-a-bad-policy versus
safety-filter-under-a-competent-policy, and only the current data speaks to
the former. During earlier stochastic-exploration training rollouts (Sec.~V,
same environment, same filter), all three robots were repeatedly observed to
leave the spawn region and traverse several meters (e.g.\ tb2 reaching global
$(2.9, 1.7)$), indicating the filter does not categorically prevent movement;
the deterministic evaluation policy specifically fails to reproduce that
exploration.

\subsection{Ablations}
Not run in this iteration of the experiment (short training budget was the
binding constraint uncovered instead); left for future work alongside a longer
training run:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No CBF:} expected collision rate at or above the raw policy's
        LiDAR-violation rate, since no filter would intervene.
  \item \textbf{No consensus:} expected redundancy increase as robots
        re-cover teammates' already-explored cells without belief sharing.
  \item \textbf{No GNN (fixed-size MLP):} the GNN variant is confirmed to run
        unchanged at $N=5$ and $N=10$ without dimension-mismatch failures
        (\texttt{test\_gnn\_scale\_invariance}); a fixed-size baseline would be
        expected to fail outright at $N \neq 3$.
\end{itemize}

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%==============================================================================
MARS separates coordination from safety: a CTDE MAPPO policy with a
permutation-invariant GNN encoder is designed to learn scalable cooperative
coverage, a dual-lookahead CBF filter constrains every commanded velocity
regardless of policy quality, and a range-limited consensus protocol
decentralizes the coverage map. Under a 15-iteration training budget, MAPPO's
deterministic policy underperforms both a Random Walk and a Frontier Heuristic
baseline on area coverage ($10.4\%$ vs.\ $18.1\%$ and $21.1\%$) and the
CBF/ACAS filter alone cannot compensate for a policy that fails to move away
from its spawn point. We view the working, tested safety and consensus
infrastructure and the honestly negative MAPPO coverage result as the
contribution of this iteration, and identify training budget, exploration
schedule, and entropy-collapse mitigation as the concrete next steps toward a
competent deterministic policy. Future work includes a substantially longer
training run before re-benchmarking, hardware transfer to physical TurtleBot3
robots, larger heterogeneous teams, and learned communication over the
consensus channel.
