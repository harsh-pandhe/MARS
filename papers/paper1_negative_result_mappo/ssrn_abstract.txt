Title: Diagnosing a Low-Displacement Failure Mode in MAPPO for Multi-Robot Area Coverage

Abstract (SSRN form field):
We train a centralized-critic MAPPO policy for three-robot area coverage in ROS 2/Gazebo under a modest compute budget (about 31,500 environment transitions) and compare it with a non-learned Frontier Heuristic (Voronoi partitioning, A*, behavior tree) and a Random Walk baseline, all behind the same Control Barrier Function safety filter. On the training environment (10 episodes per condition), the heuristic (38.6% median coverage) and Random Walk (29.6%) both outperform MAPPO (14.5%), which moves only about 1.5 m per robot per episode. A single-episode exploratory matrix across four unseen environments shows the same ranking but is not statistically tested. A three-arm reward ablation (baseline, lower collision penalty, higher discovery bonus) is inconclusive: we discovered that our Gazebo evaluation harness is not deterministic even for a fixed checkpoint and seed, caught a pseudoreplication error in our own first analysis (nine evaluation episodes per arm are not nine independent samples), and, with the correct unit of independence (three training seeds), neither manipulation reaches p < 0.05 (p = 0.054 and p = 0.10). A descriptive efficiency metric suggests the higher discovery bonus improves movement efficiency rather than distance travelled, but this is not confirmed. We report the result, the evaluation non-determinism, and the statistical correction in full, and we scope the claim to this configuration, task, and budget rather than to MARL in general.

Keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning; MAPPO; multi-robot coverage; negative results; reproducibility; control barrier functions
